# Hebrew Numbers — 1 to 20

A quick-reference sheet. Two kinds of number:

- **Cardinals** — the counting numbers (one, two, three…). Used for quantities.
- **Ordinals** — position in a sequence (first, second, third…). Distinct words exist only up to 10th; from 11th on you use the cardinal, prefixed with **ha-** for "the" (e.g. *ha-achad asar* = the eleventh).

Each number has a **masculine** and **feminine** form, chosen to agree with the noun being counted.

## Things worth knowing

- **The gender endings look backwards.** For 3–10, the *masculine* cardinal carries the ‑a ending (*shlosha*, *arba'a*) while the *feminine* is the short bare form (*shalosh*, *arba*). This is correct — it's the opposite of the usual Hebrew pattern, and it catches everyone out.
- **Twenty is genderless** — *esrim* is used for both.
- **"The eleventh," "the twelfth"…** — just prefix *ha-* to the cardinal: *ha-achad asar*, *ha-shneim asar*.
- Transliterations use a simple scheme; *ch* is the guttural sound (as in *Bach*), and the apostrophe marks a slight break between vowels.
